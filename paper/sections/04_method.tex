\subsection{Frozen candidates}
As summarized in Fig.~\ref{fig:method}, \method retains the full-expression
Grounding DINO candidate generator.  The
complete expression always enters the text encoder, transformer, decoder, and
box predictor; neither a canonical noun nor a generic ``object'' prompt
replaces this geometry path.  This design follows the diagnosis in
Sec.~\ref{sec:problem}: broad adaptation would mix representation learning
with deployment-score learning even though compatible geometry is already
available.  It also keeps boxes and base query statistics identical across
Admission-input and ownership controls.

\subsection{Category-conditioned Admission}
Let $h_i$ be a frozen final-decoder query and $u$ the category cue.  The shared
Admission surface computes
$\bar u=P_{\theta_A}(u)$ and
$a_i=\alpha\cos(W_{\theta_A}h_i,\bar u)$, where the logit scale $\alpha$ and
the cue encoder preceding $P$ are frozen.  Scores are mean--variance
standardized and clipped within the valid-candidate mask, yielding
$\tilde a_i$.  The deployed relative-margin gate is
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{E}_{\gamma}
  =\{i\in\mathcal{M}:\max_{j\in\mathcal{M}}\tilde a_j-\tilde a_i\leq\gamma\},
  \qquad \gamma=3.
  \label{eq:admission}
\end{equation}
The maximum-score valid query is necessarily eligible.  Nevertheless, the
implementation has no fallback box: a missing valid candidate, a non-finite
score, or an empty set fails closed.  Eligible rank scores remain bitwise equal
to the frozen ranker; ineligible scores are lexicographically demoted below the
floating-point predecessor of its valid minimum.  Thus the selected query is
in $\mathcal{E}_{\gamma}$ and within-set order is unchanged.

We instantiate $u$ in three capacity-matched routes.  \arrowv encodes a visual
support exemplar with a frozen patch backbone.  \arrowt encodes a sealed
canonical category phrase with the frozen language encoder, without rerunning
the image decoder.  \arrown uses a fixed dense category-agnostic sentinel that
is transformed by the same trainable Admission surface.  All three routes use
the same 268,167 trainable parameters and the same candidate mask, gate, and
losses.

\paragraph{Supervision-only auxiliary carrier.}
A small MLP predicts
$\delta_i^A$ from $[\tilde a_i,\tilde r_i,\tilde a_i\tilde r_i]$ and forms
$r_i^{\rm aux}=r_i+\delta_i^A$.  Its objective combines category-complete
instance margins, repair/preserve margins on $r^{\rm aux}$, preservation of
the expression-bound target, and residual regularization (Supplement).
Neither $\delta^A$ nor $r^{\rm aux}$ enters Eq.~\eqref{eq:admission} or deployed
Ranking: it is a \emph{gradient carrier}, not a decision variable.  The runtime
computes it for compatibility, but the gate and rank route ignore its output.

\subsection{Complete-expression Ranking}
Within the eligible set, the frozen positive-only ranker uses the complete
expression:
\begin{equation}
  r_i=R_{\theta_R}(q_i,e),\qquad
  i^*=\argmaxop_{i\in\mathcal{E}_{\gamma}} r_i.
\end{equation}
It learns a candidate residual over the frozen full-expression score.  On
positive images, row-balanced soft-margin terms repair a wrong base top-1 and
constrain an already-correct positive--negative gap to shrink by at most 0.02,
with margin 0.05, temperature 0.1, and residual weight $10^{-3}$ (supplement).
Ranking is then frozen before Admission and Rejection.  Replacing $e$ by the
canonical noun while holding boxes and eligibility fixed changes 37.85\% of
top-1 queries and lowers validation Acc@0.5 from 72.46\% to 61.72\%.  Thus
Admission constructs the comparison domain, while attributes and relations
remain owned by Ranking.

\subsection{Sample-level Rejection}
For query $i$, let $s_i$ be the mean of the sigmoid-transformed logits over
every token of the complete expression.  From detached $[h_i,s_i]$, the
rejector builds a query feature and pools it with
$\operatorname{softmax}(s_i/0.01)$.  It concatenates that pool with
six detached-score statistics: maximum, top-3 mean, mean, standard deviation,
top-1--top-2 margin, and normalized pooling entropy.  An MLP returns one shared
image--expression offset $g$, giving the sample score
$c=\max_i(s_i+g)=\max_i s_i+g$; Admission and Ranking scores are not inputs.
Deployment emits $b_{i^*}$ when $c\geq\tau$ and abstains otherwise.  We call
$c$ a non-relative or sample-level rejection score: ``absolute'' means that it
does not reorder candidate instances, not that it is a universally calibrated
probability.  Training uses every paired negative against a detached exact
positive 5th-percentile threshold, a 512-entry recent-positive queue after
256 entries, and a one-sided positive-score trust term; the paired-margin
coefficient is zero.  The supplement gives the exact loss.  Deployment
threshold $\tau$ is fixed on sealed internal calibration before external
evaluation.

\subsection{Exclusive parameter ownership}
Let $\Theta_A$, $\Theta_R$, and $\Theta_C$ denote the trainable owners of the
three duties in their respective phases.  \method enforces
\begin{equation}
\Theta_A\cap\Theta_R=\Theta_A\cap\Theta_C
=\Theta_R\cap\Theta_C=\varnothing,
\end{equation}
and, for duties $d\neq d'$, $\nabla_{\Theta_{d'}}\mathcal{L}_d=0$.  The latter
is an autograd/ownership contract, not an empirical claim that a shared
gradient happened to be small.  Our controlled comparison separates output
count from feature ownership: one shared scalar, separate outputs over a
shared trainable feature, isolated owners with interleaved exposure, and
isolated owners with phased exposure.  In ARROW-U2, the evidence supports
ownership isolation; it does not show an independent benefit from phased
scheduling.  Section~\ref{sec:results} separately tests whether this advantage
survives a capacity-matched transfer to a stronger query representation.
