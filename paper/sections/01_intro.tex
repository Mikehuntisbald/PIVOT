\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Referring-expression comprehension (REC) usually assumes that every request
denotes exactly one object.  Deployment cannot: an expression may be stale,
contradictory, or unsupported by the scene, and returning the least-wrong box
is then a failure.  Generalized benchmarks expose this case explicitly through
no-target examples~\cite{he2023grec,ding2026grex}.  Reliable grounding must
therefore localize \emph{selectively}.

A detector-style grounder produces text-conditioned candidate queries and
scores~\cite{liu2025groundingdino}, but one matching score is often asked to
perform three different decisions.  \emph{Admission} determines which
candidates may compete; \emph{Ranking} selects the admitted instance that best
matches the complete expression; \emph{Abstention} decides whether any result
should be emitted.  Ranking is relative and always has a maximum on a nonempty
set.  Abstention is sample-level and must suppress that maximum when no valid
referent exists.  Conflating the decisions hides both what failed and which
parameters caused the failure.

We introduce \method, a factorized policy over frozen grounding candidates.
An optional category cue controls Admission, the complete expression retains
candidate generation and within-set Ranking, and detached candidate evidence
drives Abstention without changing the selected box.  Figure~\ref{fig:teaser}
shows the three resulting failure tests: a compatible candidate can already
exist while Ranking is wrong; changing only the category cue should change who
is admitted; and a no-target sample still has a ranking winner that should not
be emitted.  The released \textbf{ARROW-U2} uses visual-support Admission and
isolated Ranking/Abstention owners, but factorization does not prescribe one
parameter topology for every representation.

Whether Ranking and Abstention should share adaptation parameters is an
empirical property of the representation.  On the frozen representation used
by ARROW-U2, a capacity- and compute-matched shared owner experiences recurrent
negative-gradient events and substantially damages Ranking; hard isolation
protects the localization route.  On a strong RefCOCO-tuned MM-GDINO trunk,
capacity-matched wide sharing is competitive with isolation.  A matched TN10
continuation deepens the negative cosine tail without creating an isolation
benefit, showing that gradient conflict alone is not a sufficient allocation
rule.  The robust conclusion is thus conditional: decision semantics should be
factorized, while parameter allocation should be validated at the deployed
endpoints rather than prescribed universally.

Admission requires a separate functional test.  Standard accuracy is similar
for learned-null, category-text, and visual-support cues, yet a fresh
bidirectional category-switch panel separates them sharply.  This shows why an
endpoint can remain accurate even when its nominal Admission input does not
control category routing.  External negatives add a complementary result:
positive--negative ordering improves on FineCops-Ref and restricted gRefCOCO,
whereas a threshold frozen on the source domain under-accepts external
positives.

Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Decision factorization.}  We formulate selective single-target
  grounding as Admission, Ranking, and Abstention, with explicit deployed
  semantics and failure attribution.
  \item \textbf{Representation-dependent interference controls.}  Matched
  shared and isolated owners show when hard separation protects Ranking and
  when a strong representation supports effective sharing.
  \item \textbf{Functional and external validation.}  Category-switch
  interventions reveal Admission controllability, while two external negative
  constructions separate transferable rejection ordering from calibration.
\end{enumerate}
