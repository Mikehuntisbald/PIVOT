\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\method treats selective visual grounding as a deployed decision problem
rather than a monolithic matching score.  A frozen foundation model supplies
candidate geometry; Admission constructs a category-eligible set, the complete
expression ranks within that set, and an independent rejector decides whether
to emit the winner.  A shared-scalar control exposes a measured gradient
conflict; exclusive trainable ownership removes the cross-duty autograd path
that separate output heads alone retain.  The resulting policy
improves both localization and rejection and reveals distinct controllability
for generic, textual, and visual category cues.  FineCops is descriptive;
paired gRefCOCO confirms rejection-ordering transfer.  Fixed-threshold shift
identifies the next problem clearly: learning a domain-stable operating point,
not merely a better within-domain ordering.
