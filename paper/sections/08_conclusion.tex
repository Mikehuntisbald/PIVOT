\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\method treats selective visual grounding as three decisions rather than one
matching score.  A frozen model supplies candidates; Admission constructs the
comparison set; the complete expression ranks within it; and sample-level
Abstention decides whether the winner should be emitted.  This factorized
policy improves held-out localization and verified rejection, while category
interventions expose responsibility hidden by standard accuracy.

Owner controls yield a conditional mechanism.  Capacity-matched sharing can
damage Ranking on a conflict-prone representation, where isolation is
protective; strong MM-GDINO representations instead support competitive wide
sharing, even when TN exposure deepens the measured negative-gradient tail.
Conflict is therefore diagnostic but not sufficient for choosing owner
topology.  Decision responsibilities remain distinct even when their
adaptation parameters need not be.  External results further show that
rejection ordering transfers more readily than a fixed operating threshold.
