\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Text and visual prompting for grounding.}
Open-vocabulary grounding models such as Grounding
DINO~\cite{liu2025groundingdino} use language to define detection concepts.
T-Rex2 aligns text and visual prompts~\cite{jiang2024trex2}; PET-DINO adds
visual cues and parallel prompt routes to Grounding
DINO~\cite{fu2026petdino}; DINO-X and DINOv broaden visual and in-context
prompting~\cite{ren2024dinox,li2024dinov}.  \method does not claim novelty from
accepting both text and visual inputs.  It studies their \emph{decision role}:
a category cue conditions Admission, while the complete expression retains
geometry generation and within-set Ranking.  A category-switch intervention
tests whether Admission follows its assigned cue rather than merely preserving
aggregate accuracy.

\paragraph{Referring expressions with absent targets.}
RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, and RefCOCOg support the classical exactly-one-target
setting~\cite{yu2016modelingcontext,mao2016generation}.  gRefCOCO extends the
task to zero, one, or multiple targets through GRES/GREC
formulations~\cite{liu2023gres,he2023grec,ding2026grex}.  RECANTFormer and
HieA2G explicitly model variable target cardinality
~\cite{hemanthage2024recantformer,wang2025hiea2g}; instance-aware methods align
phrases to instances or jointly predict boxes and masks
~\cite{nguyen2024instalign,dai2026instancevg}; RC-GRPO balances localization
and refusal in an MLLM policy~\cite{yang2026rcgrpo}.  FineCops-Ref probes
fine-grained positives and counterfactual negative expressions or images
~\cite{liu2024finecops}.  We neither claim full variable-cardinality grounding
nor the first no-target head.  We isolate the specialist single-target policy:
which candidates enter, which candidate wins, and whether any winner is
emitted.  Our gRefCOCO experiment is restricted to single-target versus
no-target expressions.

\paragraph{Multi-task interference and parameter allocation.}
GradNorm, PCGrad, and CAGrad alter the magnitude or direction of gradients on
shared parameters~\cite{chen2018gradnorm,yu2020pcgrad,liu2021cagrad}; adapters
and LoRA allocate task-specific capacity~\cite{houlsby2019adapters,hu2022lora}.
\method is neither a general gradient-surgery rule nor a claim that all duties
must use disjoint parameters.  Instead, it exposes separate deployed decision
surfaces and directly compares shared, capacity-matched shared, and isolated
owners.  This separates decision factorization, which is common to all
variants, from hard parameter isolation, which is one interference-control
policy.
