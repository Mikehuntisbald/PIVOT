\begin{abstract}
Modern grounding models often generate a geometrically valid candidate even
when their deployed top-1 decision is wrong.  Reliable deployment nevertheless
requires three different decisions: which candidates are category-eligible,
which admitted candidate best matches the complete expression, and whether any
result should be emitted.  We show that coupling these duties through a shared
score or trainable feature owner can be harmful.  On our validation analysis, a
frozen Grounding DINO representation contains an IoU$\geq0.5$ candidate for
every expression, while more than 27\% of examples remain mis-ranked within
the admitted set.  In a shared-scalar Admission/Rejection control, the two
training gradients have negative mean alignment in every seed; separate outputs
that retain a shared trainable feature owner do not improve calibration FPR95.
We introduce \method,
which factorizes selective grounding into \emph{Admission}, \emph{Ranking}, and
\emph{Rejection} over frozen candidates and gives every deployed surface an
exclusive parameter owner.  \method improves Test5 Acc@0.5 by
\ArrowTestGain\ points and reduces Strict-TN2031 FPR95 by
\ArrowStrictGain\ points.  A controlled ownership intervention preserves
localization while reducing FPR95 by \ArrowOwnershipStrictGain\ points.  A
fresh category-switch test separates null, category-text, and visual-support
Admission (\ArrowNullSwitch\%, \ArrowTextSwitch\%, and
\ArrowVisualSwitch\%).  FineCops-Ref gives a descriptive ordering gain and
paired gRefCOCO confirms transfer, while fixed source thresholds fail on both.
\end{abstract}
